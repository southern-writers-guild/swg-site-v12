The voice instructions in Sanity (`tarotVoiceText`) deliberately warn against reusing real outputs as examples, since the model leans on a concrete sample harder than intended instead of inventing fresh — see "Invent something wholly new every time" under COURT AND MAJOR CARD TITLES. This file is the opposite kind of record: a place for us to notice what's working, not something the model ever sees.

## Pattern notes (2026-09-08)

Rick's own read, stated directly and worth taking as a real signal rather than a guess: **the sillier the answer, the better the response.** Mechanically this tracks with "THE ONE THING THAT MATTERS MOST" in the voice instructions — a real, specific, weird answer ("its a cuke," "sour milk," "mike tyson") gives Jean-Paul something concrete and unmistakably particular to seize on; a careful or generic answer gives him nothing to grab, and the reading drifts toward the very genericness the voice rules already fight. This is also the reason the ghost-text placeholders (added 2026-09-07) matter more than they might seem to — they model the register of a good answer by example, without ever telling the user outright to be weird, which was the explicit design call made that day ("saying it kinda kills the magic").
